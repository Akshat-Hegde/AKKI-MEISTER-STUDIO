*Written 4 Nov 2026. Built on `research/creator-landscape.md` and `research/india-ecosystem.md`. Pair it with `strategy/ROADMAP.md` (the checklist) and `CONTENT-CALENDAR.md` (the next 4 weeks).*

> **⚠️ Settle these before anything goes public**
> 1. **Kids in coaching photos.** `library/sim-coaching/` includes minors. Get written parental consent before any child's face appears in a post. Otherwise use shots where they aren't identifiable, or blur them.
> 2. **The India Open figure is top 80 out of 33,000+ registered participants** (confirmed by Akki, 4 Nov 2026). It comes from the MFRL app, not a press release, so keep a screenshot of the leaderboard as proof. Your whole positioning rests on credibility, and the audience *will* check.

## The goal, in one paragraph

Become the most trusted sim racing and motorsport creator in India, with a lifestyle layer, by teaching real knowledge in every post. Turn that trust into four things: sim expo and event invitations, brand partnerships, ambassador roles, and eventually team invitations and F1 paddock access. The model is how Jimmy Broadbent, Super GT, Jardier and Adi Plays grew: a clear lane, a recurring format people come back for, real credibility, and a sim-to-real storyline. The lane is Akki's own: **the Indian driver who races, coaches and teaches the data.**

## 1. Positioning

The research found three openings that nobody in India owns:
- No premium, credible Indian sim racing educator.
- Mumbai Falcons Racing had to bring in **Track Titan (UK)** to coach India Open finalists on telemetry.
- Fanatec's new India partner says it wants creators.

Adi Plays owns **F1 fandom and lifestyle**. That makes him a collab partner, not a competitor.

### Three options

| # | One-line "why follow me" | Owns | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **"India's own sim racing coach: real laps, real data, real racecraft. Every post makes you faster."** | Education + data, the Driver61 / Jardier lane, India edition | Needs constant, accurate teaching output |
| B | **"Sim, kart, Formula Student: the engineer-driver showing how racing really works, from rig to real."** | The sim-to-real story (the Broadbent / Super GT arc) | Real-world content is expensive and irregular; hard to sustain weekly |
| C | **"Your path from a G29 in your bedroom to the India Open finals, from someone who's been through it."** | The Indian pathway and beginners | Narrow; ties the brand to one event that Akki doesn't control |

### Recommendation: **A**, with B as the storyline and C as the entry door

> **AKKI MEISTER: India's own sim racing coach. Real laps, real data, real racecraft. Every post makes you faster.**

Why A:
1. **It's the gap with the most evidence behind it.** When India's official F1 sim programme needed data coaching, it had to look abroad. Being the Indian racer who teaches data and racecraft in plain English, with Indian context (Indian hardware prices, Indian events, Indian tracks), is a clear and defensible lane.
2. **It uses every asset at once.**
   - The India Open and podiums are the proof that he can drive.
   - Coaching, including kids, shows he can teach.
   - Telemetry and Formula Student show he understands the engineering.
   - The brand makes all of it look premium.
3. **Each goal wants exactly this:**
   - Expos want hosts and coaches.
   - Fanatec and VRH want someone who can *explain* their products.
   - MFRL and F1's India programme want a knowledgeable local voice who isn't a cricketer.
   - Teams want reach into India's grassroots.
4. **Education compounds.** Saves and shares (the signals that bring new followers) come from useful posts, and a back catalogue of explainers keeps working for years.

How B and C fit in: B (karting, Formula Student, any real-car step) is the **storyline** that gives the channel a narrative arc and the lifestyle pillar. C is the **entry door**: beginner and India Open content that pulls new people in, and A then keeps them.

**Tone towards Track Titan:** never "instead of the UK guys". Say "India's own", and position as *complementary*. Track Titan is a possible partner or affiliate, not an enemy (see §6).

## 2. Content pillars and mix

| Pillar | Share | What it is | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Racecraft & technique** | 30% | Trail braking, lines, brake bias, quali vs race, defending, consistency, setups explained | Value, saves, authority |
| **2. Data & telemetry** | 20% | Reading traces, finding time, lap comparisons, ERS/battery under the 2026 regs, the follower lap reviews | The unique lane (the "India's own" proof) |
| **3. Sim to real** | 15% | Karting, Formula Student engineering + driving, sim vs real-car differences, real tracks (BIC, Kari) | Storyline, teams and paddock credibility |
| **4. The Indian scene** | 15% | India Open, events, hardware at Indian prices, leagues, people, beginners and parents | The "starting in India" goal, expo invites, Fanatec / VRH relevance |
| **5. Life at speed** | 20% | Rig life, event days, studies + racing, the people, wins and losses, F1 weekends from a driver's view | Personality, the reason people *stay*, brand-safe lifestyle for partners |

**Rule of thumb:** about half of all posts teach something (pillars 1–2). The other half shows who Akki is and where he's going (3–5). Even lifestyle posts carry one learnable line, as CLAUDE.md asks.

## 3. Signature series

Four named, repeatable formats. **The Debrief is the recurring show**, the missing piece the research identified.

### 🎙️ THE DEBRIEF: *the show* (weekly)
- **Premise:** followers send in a lap. Akki puts it next to his own (or a reference lap), finds where the time is, and gives **one** drill. Next episode opens with "last week's driver went back out…" with the before/after result.
- **Why people come back:**
  - Viewers submit laps and want to be picked.
  - Every episode has a real person, a real gap and a payoff.
  - It's the community-led loop that Jardier's league and Broadbent's annual race provide, but it runs weekly and is built on the data lane.
  - It's also a public demo of Akki's coaching, which brings coaching leads and brand interest.
- **Format:**
  - Reel / Short: 60–90 s, 1080×1920. Structure: hook ("0.6 s lost in two corners"), the driver and car, trace overlay (speed + brake/throttle), the fix, a call to submit.
  - From month 4, a monthly or fortnightly 10–15 min YouTube cut that reviews 2–3 laps in depth.
- **Submissions:**
  - A form or Instagram broadcast channel at first; Discord once it exists.
  - What to send: a lap video with the input overlay, or a telemetry export from whatever tool the driver uses (F1 games broadcast UDP telemetry that many free apps record). Include track, car or game, assists and controller or wheel.
  - Start with laps from people Akki already coaches, with their permission.
- **Ep 0 (pilot):** Akki debriefs **his own** India Open-style lap, showing what he'd change. That sets the format before any submissions arrive.
- **Day:** Saturday.

### 📐 APEX NOTES: the explainer (weekly)
- **What it is:** an Instagram carousel, 6–8 slides at 1080×1350, following the `CLAUDE.md` infographic spec (hook → one idea per slide → takeaway).
- **Topics:** one technique, setting or regulation idea at a time.
- **Accuracy:** every 🔎 fact is checked against current sources. Akki's values are labelled "my setup".
- **Reuse:** each carousel also becomes a LinkedIn document post, and every 3rd one becomes a 45 s talking-head Short.
- **Day:** Monday.

### 🏁 SIM TO TARMAC: the storyline (monthly)
- **What it is:** a mini-documentary. Take one skill from the rig and test it in the real world:
  - kart lap times before and after sim practice,
  - Formula Student setup vs F1 26 setup,
  - what BIC's corners feel like vs a sim.
- **Format:** reel / Short of 60–90 s. Once Akki has the footage habit, from month 6, an 8–12 min YouTube episode.
- **The slogan** comes straight from the brand ("SIM TO / TARMAC.").
- **Why:** it's the Broadbent / Super GT growth arc, Indian edition, and it's what teams and real-racing sponsors notice.

### ⏱️ AM HOTLAP CHALLENGE: the community event (monthly, from month 2)
- **How it works:**
  - One track and car per month, on F1 26 time trial (and later ACC).
  - Entries are a screenshot or lap video, posted in Discord, under a hashtag, or by DM.
  - Leaderboard story every Sunday; the top 3 laps get a Debrief episode.
- **Why:**
  - It's India's missing **creator-led** recurring community event (gap #7 in the research).
  - It's a ready-made slot for a brand (a prize from Fanatec India, VRH or a distributor).
  - It's Akki's first "event" of his own, the seed of hosting real ones.
- **Partners:** run it with the Indian Racing Community (IRC) or VRH once it has traction, rather than competing with them.

### Plus one recurring story slot: **SETUP SUNDAY**
- Kept from the old calendar: a Sunday story set on one piece of the rig or one setting.
- Low effort; keeps the habit and the Q&A going.
- A monthly carousel rounds it up.

## 4. Platform plan

**Real-life budget:** Akki is studying or working, so the plan assumes **about 8 hours a week**:
- 2 h filming / recording laps,
- 3 h editing and design with the templates (the Session 4 content engine is what makes this possible),
- 1 h captions and repurposing,
- 2 h community (replies, Discord, other creators' posts).

There's a **minimum viable week** for exam weeks (see below), and missing a week beats burning out.

| Platform | Role | Cadence | Start |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Home base, the discovery and community engine | 3 feed posts/week: Mon Apex Notes, Wed reel (pillar 3/4/5 rotation), Sat The Debrief. Stories 4–5 days/week, including Setup Sunday. Broadcast channel for lap submissions and drops. | Now |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Second discovery engine; builds a subscriber base for long-form | Every reel, re-exported without the IG watermark, posted the same day or next day (about 2–3/week). Titles are search-style ("How to trail brake in F1 26"). | Now |
| **YouTube long-form** | The library, search traffic, the real money and the expo / team calling card | Start **month 4 (Feb–Mar 2027)**, once The Debrief has 10+ episodes and a submission pipeline. 1 per month at first, then every 2 weeks from month 7 if it's sustainable. | Month 4 |
| **LinkedIn** | The B2B channel, for brand managers, MFRL, motorsport and FS people, engineering recruiters | 1 post/week: carousels as PDF documents, coaching and engineering lessons, event recaps, "what I learned" posts. Professional voice, same brand. | Now |
| **Discord ("AM Paddock")** | Community home for lap submissions, the Hotlap Challenge and coaching-led Q&A | Launch in **month 2–3**, alongside Hotlap Challenge #1, once there are enough engaged followers to feel alive. Until then, the IG broadcast channel does the job, and Akki stays active in the IRC Discord. | Month 2–3 |
| **Twitch / live** | Later: Hotlap Challenge finals, charity or endurance stream | Optional from month 9+. One "event" stream per quarter, not a schedule. | 9+ months |

### First long-form YouTube video: candidate titles
1. "How I finished top 80 of 33,000+ at the F1 Sim Racing India Open (and what I'd change)"
2. "I reviewed 5 of your laps. Here's the mistake all of you make" (a Debrief compilation).
3. "Sim racing in India: the complete beginner's guide (₹30k to ₹3 lakh)" 🔎 prices at the time of posting.

### Minimum viable week (exams, travel, bad weeks)
- 1 Debrief (or a re-cut of an old explainer),
- 1 story set,
- 1 Short mirror.

Nothing else. That keeps the show alive, and the show is what matters most.

## 5. The path: milestones

**Read this first:**
- Follower targets are **planning assumptions, not predictions**. Record your current baseline in `ROADMAP.md` and recalibrate after month 1.
- For brands and teams, a small, engaged, Indian, niche audience matters more than a big raw count. Adi Plays got Alpine F1 guest access at roughly 40–63k (research), on reach *into India*, not global size.

**What drives each goal:**

| Goal | Mostly depends on |
|---|---|
| Expo / event invites | **Networking** + credibility (results); audience helps but is secondary |
| Brand partnerships (product, affiliate, paid) | **Content quality + engaged niche audience** + a media kit |
| Ambassador roles | **Consistency over time** (12+ months of reliable posting and brand fit) |
| Team / paddock access | **Reach into India** + relationships + a racing or engineering story; **results** (India Open) help a lot with MFRL and F1's India programme |

### Phase 1: 0–3 months (Nov 2026 – Jan 2027) · *"Prove the format"*
- **Audience (assumption):**
  - IG 2–3k engaged followers (or 2× baseline, whichever is higher),
  - YT 300–500 subs from Shorts,
  - LinkedIn 500+ relevant connections.
- **Content:**
  - 12+ Apex Notes and 10+ Debrief episodes.
  - Sim to Tarmac #1–3.
  - Hotlap Challenge #1 (Jan).
  - India Open finals-week coverage.
- **Credibility:**
  - Leaderboard proof in hand.
  - Podium and results archive on the website and in the media kit.
  - Formula Bharat 2027 coverage (19–24 Jan 2027, Kari, per research).
- **Networking:**
  - Media kit done (Playbook Session 3).
  - Warm contact at **VRH** (offer a coaching session or event co-host).
  - First contact with **Fanatec India / Vishal Peripherals**.
  - Introduce yourself to MFRL, with content about the India Open as the opener.
  - DM **Adi Plays** with the "F1 fan vs sim racer" collab idea.
- **Unlocks:**
  - Small event slots: coach or host at a VRH event or a college fest sim event (IEEE Geekmayem-type).
  - Product seeding / loaner hardware conversations.

### Phase 2: 3–6 months (Feb – Apr 2027) · *"Become the name in Indian sim racing education"*
- **Audience (assumption):** IG 5–8k, YT 1.5–3k, Discord 150–300 members.
- **Content:**
  - YouTube long-form starts (monthly).
  - Debrief episodes include outside submissions every week.
  - Hotlap Challenge monthly, ideally co-run with IRC or VRH.
  - 1 creator collab per month (Adi Plays, an IRC league name, an Indian karting creator).
- **Credibility:**
  - Speaking or coaching slot at 1–2 events.
  - A visible result in a league (IRC / VRH / SimGrid), which comes from **racing**, not content.
- **Unlocks:**
  - **First paid or product partnership:**
    - Fanatec India, VRH, a Moza / Simagic distributor, or an Indian rig or retail brand.
    - Affiliate codes for Indian retailers (disclosed).
  - Hotlap Challenge prize sponsor.

### Phase 3: 6–12 months (May – Oct 2027) · *"Known in the room"*
- **Audience (assumption):** IG 12–25k, YT 5–10k.
- **Content:**
  - Fortnightly long-form if sustainable.
  - A season-long Sim to Tarmac arc (a real kart season or a real-car step, if budget allows).
  - Next India Open season covered as "the creator who's also racing it" ⚠️ dates TBC.
- **Credibility:**
  - **Racing:** improve on the 2026 India Open result. A finals run or better is the single biggest lever with MFRL and F1's India programme.
  - Invited (not self-pitched) to at least 1 event as host, coach or judge.
- **Unlocks:**
  - Recurring brand deal (3+ posts, a retainer or long-term product deal).
  - **Indian paddock access** (F4 India / IRL / JK Tyre / Formula Bharat media or guest passes). These are far more reachable than F1 and build the paddock portfolio.
  - First conversation about an **ambassador** role (the Coach Dave × Jardier model: a coaching or data brand, a hardware brand, or VRH).

### Phase 4: 12–24 months (Nov 2027 – Oct 2028) · *"The go-to Indian voice"*
- **Audience (assumption):** IG 40–80k, YT 20–50k. Adi-Plays-scale reach into India is where F1-team India activations start inviting creators (research precedent: Alpine × Adi Plays).
- **Content:**
  - A flagship annual event of his own: an AM Cup final at VRH, or an endurance or charity race (the Broadbent model, Indian edition).
  - Long-form becomes the core.
- **Unlocks:**
  - **Ambassador role** with a hardware or coaching brand.
  - **Expo stage** in India (host or MC for India Open rounds, gaming expos), possibly abroad (SimRacing Expo in Germany has hosted creator stages, per research).
  - **Team / F1 paddock invitation:** realistic only if reach, relationships and timing line up. Paths:
    - an F1 team's India marketing push,
    - MFRL / F1 India programme content partnerships,
    - an Indian F1-sponsor brand campaign.
  - **Not guaranteed. Plan to deserve it, not to expect it.**

## 6. Metrics: track monthly (first Sunday of each month, ~20 min)

Track these in a simple sheet. Month 1 sets the **baseline**, and "working" means trending up against it. The numbers below are rough targets to calibrate, not industry benchmarks.

| Metric | Where | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Saves + shares per post** (especially Apex Notes) | IG Insights | The north-star value signal. Saves exceed likes ÷ 10 and climb month on month. |
| **Reach to non-followers %** | IG Insights | At least half of reel reach comes from non-followers (discovery is happening). |
| **Reel / Short average watch % and 3-sec hold** | IG / YT Studio | Debrief hold rate improves episode on episode as hooks sharpen. |
| **Follower growth (IG, YT, LinkedIn)** | Platforms | Steady monthly growth; no single viral spike needed. |
| **India share of audience** | IG / YT audience tabs | India is the top country, Mumbai / Bangalore / Delhi / Pune in the top cities. That's what Indian brands buy. |
| **Debrief submissions / month** | Form / DMs / Discord | Phase 1: 5+. Phase 2: 20+. The show is alive when it never runs short of laps. |
| **Hotlap Challenge entries** | Discord / tags | Growing every month. |
| **Discord weekly active members** | Discord | Above 20% of members. |
| **Inbound DMs and emails from brands / organisers / teams** | Inbox log | The real goal metric. First inbound by month 4–6. |
| **Coaching enquiries** | DMs / form | The value is landing (and it's income). |
| **Events attended / spoken at** | Log | 1+ per month in Phase 2+. |
| **Output consistency** | Calendar | 90%+ of planned posts shipped. |

**Red flags** (adjust if any persist 2 months):
- Saves are falling (the content isn't useful enough).
- Non-follower reach is under 20% (hooks or topics are wrong).
- No Debrief submissions (the call to action is too hard; make submitting easier).
- More than 2 missed weeks (the plan is too big; cut to the minimum viable week).

## 7. Risks and what to avoid

1. **Getting a fact wrong.** This is the expert lane, so one wrong ERS or regulation claim costs more than ten good posts earn.
   - Every 🔎 gets checked with sources.
   - Personal values are labelled "my setup".
   - Corrections go in a pinned comment or story, not deleted.
2. **Overstating results.** Always say exactly "top 80 of 33,000+ registered". Never round up. Keep screenshots of every result.
3. **Burnout and inconsistency.** The research pattern is that creators who last 6+ months win. Use the minimum viable week, batch production (Session 5), and templates. Don't add platforms ahead of the plan.
4. **Copying.** Learn the *mechanics*, not the look:
   - Broadbent: the annual event.
   - Jardier: community and setups.
   - Driver61: teaching clarity.
   - Never copy their formats' names, styles or catchphrases.
5. **Drifting into F1 news and fandom.** That's Adi Plays' lane and a crowded one. F1 race weekends are fine *through a driver's lens* ("what Verstappen did at T1 and how you do it in the sim"), not as reaction content.
6. **Bad-mouthing anyone** (Track Titan, other creators, organisers). The Indian scene is small; today's competitor is tomorrow's event organiser or partner.
7. **Minors on camera.** Parental consent, no identifying details, never in partnership content without separate consent.
8. **Undisclosed brand content.** Indian advertising rules (ASCI influencer guidelines) require clear disclosure labels on paid or gifted content. Use "#ad / Paid partnership" every time. 🔎 Check the current ASCI wording before the first deal.
9. **Early exclusivity.** Don't sign a cheap exclusive hardware deal in Phase 1–2; brands switch creators and creators switch brands (Fanatec → Moza / Simagic, per research). Prefer non-exclusive or category-limited deals until Phase 3.
10. **IP and music.** Don't use F1 marks or logos in series names or branding; game footage in commentary or education is normal, but don't present it as official. Licensed music only, low in the mix (per CLAUDE.md).
11. **Spending on gear to make content.** The rig is good enough; the lane is knowledge, not hardware reviews (Boosted Media's lane is capital-intensive, per research).
12. **Platform dependence.** Build owned channels (Discord, an email list later, the website) so an Instagram algorithm change doesn't wipe out the business.
