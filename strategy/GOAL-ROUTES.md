## 1. F1 paddock access

**How it really works:** F1 media passes are issued by the FIA through its Accreditation Management System (AMS). The path is: register an organisation, get the account approved, create applicant profiles, then apply per event. Filming and social video requests are routed to **F1's media rights department**, and some promoters ask for a copyright agreement. Approval is discretionary: the FIA can refuse even when every criterion is met. It weighs professionalism, experience and **previous coverage**. Race promoters (e.g. Monza) favour outlets with substantial, consistent coverage and exclude sites on free platforms.

Sources: [FIA 2024 F1 accreditation guidelines (via GP Canada)](https://gpcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2024_fia_formula_1_world_championship_-_accreditation_guidelines_final.pdf) · [FIA 2025 guidelines summary (Baku City Circuit)](https://www.bakucitycircuit.com/news/fia-announces-media-accreditation-guidelines-for-the-2025-formula-1-world-championship) · [F1 Academy media info](https://f1academy.com/About/3Wfusl2ppxfkqPqhWfgXAF/f1-academy-media-info) · [Monza media accreditation](https://www.monzanet.it/en/?p=4293) · [Las Vegas GP broadcast/podcast form](https://form.jotform.com/243435419979168)

**What this means for you (realistic order):**
1. **Year 1: become "previous coverage".** A consistent body of accurate F1-adjacent work, posted weekly: APEX NOTES, DEBRIEF and the Indian sim scene. This is the portfolio the FIA criteria ask for.
2. **Own domain + site** (not a free blog platform) with an archive of articles and race-weekend analysis. The Monza rule excludes free-platform sites. 🔎
3. **Start where creators are admitted by name.** F1 Academy asks content creators for visibility, viewership and engagement across platforms. National and Asian series (see §2) are also more open to creators than F1 is.
4. **The more common way creators actually get in: an invitation, not a pass.** Teams, sponsors and F1 itself invite creators to campaigns and fan events. That's what §3 and §4 lead to. A paddock invite from a brand partner is the realistic first paddock day.
5. Only after 1–4: apply through AMS for a specific event, with links to your coverage. 🔎

## 4. Invitations from teams

Teams invite people who are **already useful to their audience**, especially in a growth market like India:
- **Their esports arms first.** F1 Sim Racing / F1 Esports teams and the India Open ecosystem. Your top-80 result plus coaching is the hook. Offer India-market content (Hindi/English explainer of their driver's onboard, made with the explainer template).
- **Tag precisely and stay accurate.** A team's social staff will notice a creator whose breakdowns are correct; they will also notice a mistake. Never invent numbers (template rule).
- **Formula Student** is your engineering credibility. FS India events and team sponsors are realistic first "team" partnerships.

## 5. What to measure (monthly; see `STRATEGY.md` §6)

Saves and shares per post (teaching content is judged on saves), Reels 3-second hold, profile visits → follows, India share of audience, and inbound brand/organiser messages. Instagram's own guidance favours **original content and original audio** for educational posts, so no reposts and no other apps' watermarks. Check the Professional Dashboard → *Best practices* and the [@creators](https://www.instagram.com/creators/) account for the current rules 🔎. Secondary summaries: [Buffer, Reels 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-instructions-and-ideas-for-small-businesses/), [Rival IQ](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/best-practices-instagram-reels/).
